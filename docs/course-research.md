# Beginner acoustic guitar (chords + strumming): course research and a proposed 30-lesson syllabus

Prepared 2026-11-19 for the guitar-karaoke practice app (HTML, microphone checks).
Language: English, written so it can be translated to Hebrew. Source URLs are given per claim.

**Evidence labels**
- [V] = verified in a source I could open or that a search returned.
- [P] = my professional or design judgment, or from memory. Not verified in this research.
- [W] = weak source (blog or commercial), use with caution.

**Limits of this research**
- JustinGuitar's own pages returned HTTP 403, so I only have search snippets and community posts about them.
- I found no published lesson-by-lesson lists for Fender Play, Guitar Tricks, Marty Music, Hal Leonard or Mel Bay.
- Where I could not verify something, the text says so.

## 1. What well-known beginner courses do

### 1.1 JustinGuitar Beginner Grade 1 and 2 (the best-documented structure)
- Grade 1 teaches the eight essential open chords, the most common strumming patterns and easy songs. [V] https://justinguitar.com/begin and https://community.justinguitar.com/c/beginner-guitar-course-grade-one/34
- Module 1 is "A and D chords and your first song". Module 2 is "Rhythm and chord changes plus your first riff". Module 3 adds a finger-stretching exercise and a strumming pattern. [V] https://community.justinguitar.com/c/beginner-guitar-course-grade-one/a-d-chords-your-first-song/136 and https://community.justinguitar.com/c/beginner-guitar-course-grade-one/learn-your-first-guitar-riff-more/103
- Chord order from the community-posted lesson sequence: A, then E, then A-D-E changes using anchor fingers, then "One Minute Changes". Am, Em, Dm, C and G come later (G at roughly stage 3). [V, from snippets] https://community.justinguitar.com/c/beginner-guitar-course-classic/39 and https://classcentral.com/classroom/youtube-justinguitar-grade-1-beginner-course-56096
- Strumming starts with easy 8th-note strumming and comes before barre chords. [V] https://justinguitar.com/begin
- Grade 1 pass requirements, as written in the community guide:
  - play the 8 essential chords from memory, in any order;
  - play **30 chord changes in one minute** for a pairing;
  - Justin supplies a list of the 25 most useful changes to practise. [V] https://community.justinguitar.com/t/how-to-pass-grade-1/2478
  - The one-minute figure is quoted from a student's guide. I did not see Justin's own page.
- Songs arrive immediately: the first module already ends with a first song, and the paid "Beginner Songbook 1" collects easy songs. [V] https://justinguitar.com/store/beginner-songbook-1
- Takeaway: the "one-minute change" drill (count clean changes in 60 s) is the standard objective measure. A measurable target of about 30 per minute is used as the Grade 1 gate.

### 1.2 Fender Play
- Structure is Path > Level > Course > Lesson. The order is a guide and not a requirement. [V] https://play-support.fender.com/hc/en-us/articles/44538600109723-What-order-should-I-take-the-courses-in and https://lausd-instructor.fender.com/hc/en-us/articles/360043858892
- Lesson length: chord lessons 1-3 minutes, song lessons 8-10 minutes. [V] https://play-support.fender.com/hc/en-us/articles/44538598249755-How-long-are-the-lessons
- Songs are arranged and abridged for the learner's level, sometimes with a changed key, and placed along the path to apply skills in context. [V] https://lausd-instructor.fender.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048940851-Different-Types-of-Song-Lessons
- A Guitar World review of 8 weeks of Fender Play shows the micro-lesson style. [V] https://www.guitarworld.com/features/i-used-fender-play-for-8-weeks-to-learn-the-guitar-from-scratch-heres-how-i-got-on
- Takeaway: very short instruction clips (1-3 min) followed by practice, with songs adapted to the learner.

### 1.3 Yousician
- 10 playing levels, divided into themes (cowboy, moveable and barre chords, strumming, riffs). [V] https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/206930639-How-the-guitar-learning-path-works
- Reviewers put levels 1-5 at beginner and 6-10 at intermediate. [V] https://beginnerguitarhq.com/yousician-guitar-review
- Yousician's blog lists E, D, C, Am and Em as good starting chords, and recommends E, Em and Am as a first three. [V] https://yousician.com/blog/learn-to-play-guitar
- The app uses the microphone to give real-time feedback, in a game format. It can feel boring to people who already know the basics. [V] https://www.guitarworld.com/reviews/yousician-review
- Takeaway: it validates the "mic-checked game loop" approach. Weakness: it is rigid.

### 1.4 Andy Guitar
- The beginner course covers open chords, 4th and 8th-note strumming and simple songs. [V] https://andyguitar.co.uk/courses/beginner-course
- The free 10-day starter course goes: first riff, two-chord songs, four-chord songs, then "Open chords and the most common strumming ever" as lesson 9 of 10. [V] https://classcentral.com/course/youtube-10-day-guitar-starter-course-absolute-beginner-start-here-56045
- The first module opens with tuning, chord diagrams and chord sheets. It has a "practice routine for Level 1" video. [V] https://www.andyguitar.co.uk/videos/07-practice-routine-for-level-1
- The beginner course ends with a "consolidate before moving on" finale lesson. [V] https://www.andyguitar.co.uk/lessons/9-finale-what-to-know-before-moving-on
- Takeaway: the D chord comes first, songs come very early (two-chord songs), and there is an explicit consolidation checkpoint.

### 1.5 Guitar Tricks
- The Fundamentals 1 course begins with "Getting familiar with your instrument". [V] https://guitartricks.com/course/fundamentals1
- A third-party article describes its order as notes, then power and open chords across Fundamentals 1-2. This is that author's characterisation, not Guitar Tricks' own statement. [W] https://www.guitarchalk.com/learn-guitar-chords-in-order/
- The exact lesson order is not verified.

### 1.6 Marty Music, Hal Leonard Guitar Method, Mel Bay Modern Guitar Method
- **Marty Music:** I found no verifiable lesson list. [not verified]
- **Hal Leonard Guitar Method Book 1:** 48 pages, aimed at acoustic or electric beginners. It uses well-known public-domain songs such as "When the Saints Go Marching In" and "Yankee Doodle". Chord order was not found. [V partially] https://www.halleonard.com/product/699010/hal-leonard-guitar-method-book-1
- **Mel Bay Modern Guitar Method Grade 1:** 48 pages. The book is song-driven and uses folk and classical pieces. It introduces chord melody concepts. [V partially] https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/title/mel-bay-s-modern-guitar-method-grade-1-sheet-music/1738764
- Both books are note-reading-first print methods and weaker for chord and strum training.
- Takeaway: public-domain folk songs are the traditional vehicle for first songs. This supports our PD-only approach.

### 1.7 Cross-course consensus (what the syllabus copies)
1. Start with 2-3 easy chords (Em, Am, E, A, D). Do not start with F or barre chords. [V: Yousician, JustinGuitar]
2. Train chord changes with a timed counted drill (the one-minute change). [V: JustinGuitar]
3. Teach strumming as 4th-note downs, then 8th-note down-up, then one all-purpose pattern. [V: Andy Guitar, JustinGuitar]
4. First song within about the first week or two, using 2-3 chords. [V: JustinGuitar, Andy Guitar]
5. Short sessions (Fender 1-3 minute micro-lessons; JustinGuitar practice routines usually 15-30 min) and periodic consolidation checkpoints. [V: Fender, Andy Guitar]
6. The path is flexible. We add mastery gates but allow skipping ahead. [V: Fender]

## 2. Research on learning and practice (what the evidence supports)

For each topic: finding, strength, and the design rule it produces. Music-specific evidence is thin, so most rules are extrapolated from motor-learning research.

### 2.1 Spaced (distributed) practice
- General motor-learning evidence favours distributed over massed practice for retention, but music-specific evidence is thin (under 10% of the spacing literature is on motor tasks, almost none on music). [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5553926
- Piano sequences learned with a 24-hour gap produced fewer errors than 5-minute or 6-hour gaps (Simmons). [V, within that review above]
- Discrete versus continuous skills respond differently. Lee and Genovese (1988) argued that massed practice mostly changes in-session performance and not long-term learning. [V] https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/1988-lee.pdf
- A 2026 study found that a short inter-trial interval helped memory consolidation for serial motor skills. [V, snippet only] https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40778584/
- Learners prefer massed practice even when it works worse. [V, snippet]
- **Rule:** use short daily sessions and revisit each skill at expanding intervals (1, 3, 7, 14 days). Do not rely on one long session.

### 2.2 Deliberate practice
- Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Romer (1993) found that top violinists had accumulated about 10,000 hours of solitary practice by age 20. [V] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6731745/
- The effect is smaller than originally claimed. A double-blind replication questioned the method (retrospective estimates, unblinded raters) and the definition of "deliberate". [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731745
- **Rule:** the useful part is the structure, not the hours: specific goals, immediate feedback, work at the edge of ability. The app enforces this with a mic pass criterion per drill.

### 2.3 Challenge point and feedback frequency
- Guadagnoli and Lee (2004), Challenge Point Framework: the optimal difficulty depends on skill level. Harder practice lowers practice performance but can benefit long-term learning. [V] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenge_point_framework
- The specific feedback-frequency prediction was not verified from the text I could open. [not verified]
- **Rule (P):** beginners get frequent, simple feedback (right or wrong per attempt). As they improve, show summary feedback after a set of attempts only, and raise difficulty when 80-90% success is reached.

### 2.4 Slow practice and tempo ramping
- Learning shifts the whole speed-accuracy function, and gradual speed increases are the common recommendation. No study directly proves a fixed "slow then fast" schedule is best. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3404800 and https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/the-speed-accuracy-trade-off
- **Rule (P):** start every pattern at 50-60 BPM and raise by 5 BPM only after a clean pass (accuracy gate). Never raise tempo on a failed attempt.

### 2.5 Interleaved versus blocked practice
- Interleaving typically hurts in-session performance and helps retention (contextual interference). [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4989027
- Carter and Grahn (2016), advanced clarinetists: interleaved pieces were rated better. Results varied across raters. Mixed results in younger players. [V] https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01251/text
- No guitar study found. [V]
- **Rule:** block a new chord until it first passes (lessons 2-3 of any chord), then interleave: practise chord pairs and random chord orders in the second half of every session.

### 2.6 Mental practice
- Mental practice improves performance in pianists, but less than physical practice. Auditory imagery was linked to anticipation and motor imagery to movement velocity. [V] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3747442/
- Kinesthetic imagery is described as a method for rehearsing away from the instrument. [V] https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3680781/
- The classic meta-analysis on mental practice (Driskell, Copper and Moran 1994) was named in results but not opened. [partially verified]
- **Rule:** a 30-60 second "see and hear the next chord shape" pause before each hard transition drill. It is supplementary and never replaces playing.

### 2.7 Chunking
- Verwey's dual-processor model: sequences are integrated into chunks that can be triggered as a whole. Chunk boundaries show as longer response times. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6433800
- Older adults show reduced chunking, which is relevant for adult beginners. [V, snippet]
- **Rule:** teach a chord as one visual shape (a chunk), and teach a strum pattern as 1-2 bar chunks. Drill the chord pair or the pattern bar as a unit.

### 2.8 Sleep consolidation
- Walker et al. (2002) found additional gains in a finger-tapping sequence after sleep, specific to the learned sequence. [V] https://walkerlab.berkeley.edu/reprints/Walker et al._Neuron_2002.pdf
- Later work argues sleep stabilises rather than enhances. Interference shortly after training can block 24-hour gains. [V] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4412892
- **Rule (P):** practise the new skill in the evening or just before bed when possible, and avoid practising a very similar competing skill straight afterwards. At minimum, expect the next day's attempt to be better. The app should say so, which supports motivation.

### 2.9 Rhythm, entrainment and metronome use
- Sensorimotor synchronisation: musicians tap more consistently than non-musicians. [V] https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-012-0371-2
- I found no systematic study showing metronome practice itself improves learners' timing. [V, absence stated]
- **Rule (P):** use an audible click plus a visible beat. Fade the click out in later lessons (play 2 bars with click, then 2 bars without). Score timing against the click and against the player's own steady tempo.

### 2.10 Ear versus notation
- A recorder study with children found the ear-first group's sound was rated higher than the notation group's. [V] https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-music-education
- Learning by ear did not hurt later notation reading in middle-schoolers. Evidence on adult beginners is thin. [V]
- Green's and McPherson's work says emphasising notation without playing by ear can restrict musicianship. [V] https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/1479500/1/RB19_Playing_by_Ear_in_the_Instrumental_Lesson_Green.pdf
- **Rule:** chord diagrams and lyrics-with-chords only, no staff notation. Learning from a sung melody (the Hebrew songs) is an ear-first context.

### 2.11 Why adult beginners quit
- Adult amateurs are put off by regimented practice that does not fit their lives. Music-making can feel "attractive yet unattainable". [V] https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/101249/1/-BME-S0265051716000152a.pdf
- Reduced perceived ability, lack of enjoyment and social withdrawal drive dropout (Kokotsaki and Hallam, as quoted). [V] https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/110520/1/Violin%20in%20the%20Attic%20M%26L%20Handbook%20chapter.pdf
- Adults using piano apps report frustration from instructional and technical limits. [V] https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/226859
- The "90% quit in year one" figure (attributed to Fender) is unverified. [W] https://tonypolecastro.com/?p=2164120
- **Rule:** short sessions (10-15 min), visible progress, a real song early, a forgiving "streak" that allows rest days, and microphone error messages that are specific and kind.

### 2.12 Finger pain and calluses
- Calluses take about 2-4 weeks of regular playing. [V, consistent across sources] https://yousician.com/blog/guitar-calluses and https://www.hellosimply.com/blog/?p=11775
- Fender suggests 10-15 minutes a day at first. Frequent short sessions are better than long ones for callus growth. Stop at open wounds or serious irritation. Lighter strings help. [V] https://www.fender.com/articles/techniques/how-to-minimize-finger-pain-playing-guitar and https://ledgernote.com/columns/guitar-guru/guitar-calluses/
- **Rule:** cap week 1-2 sessions at 12-15 min, with a "rest your fingers" message. Suggest extra-light (10-47 or 11-52) strings.

## 3. Common beginner problems and the fixes to build in

All of this is standard teaching practice and mostly [P], consistent with the course sources above.

| Problem | Cause | Fix and how the app detects it |
|---|---|---|
| Buzzing or dead notes | Finger too far from the fret, flat finger muting a neighbour string, too little pressure | "Pick each string" arpeggio check: the mic must hear 4-6 distinct clear pitches (per chord). Show which string is missing. |
| Slow chord changes | Looking at the fretboard, lifting all fingers, no chunking | One-minute change drill (count clean changes in 60 s). Anchor fingers (A-D-E per JustinGuitar). Ghost-fingering on the spot (mental practice). |
| Muting (accidentally or intentionally) | Fingertips touch other strings | Strum a chord and check that all expected strings sound. Teach intentional muting later as a rhythmic tool (lesson 22). |
| Strumming hand stops when the left hand changes | Attention shifts to fretting | Keep the strumming arm moving constantly (like a pendulum). Drill: "Strum the old chord through the change, even if the new one is wrong." Score: rhythm hits stay on the beat. |
| Counting | Timing drifts | Count aloud "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and". The app plays a click and a voice count, then fades the count out. |
| Tension | Squeezing the neck and shoulders | Prompt a "shake out" break every 5 minutes. |

## 4. App design principles distilled

1. Session = 12-15 minutes (first 2 weeks), then 15-20. Structure: 2 min warm-up, 3 min review (spaced), 7 min new material, 3 min song.
2. Every drill has a mic pass criterion, a tempo, and a target number of repetitions. Fail twice, then offer an easier variant (slower, fewer chords) instead of repeating.
3. Mastery gates every 6 lessons (checkpoints). A learner who fails a checkpoint twice gets review lessons, not a block.
4. Songs: a real song every 4-6 lessons, first at lesson 6. Songs reuse only chords already learned.
5. Spaced review: each chord and pattern comes back at +1, +3, +7 and +14 days as a 1-2 minute warm-up. Missed review lowers its priority only until the learner scores 80% again.
6. Chord recognition (mic): use chroma (pitch-class) vector matching against the chord's pitch classes, using 3 consecutive frames after the strum onset. Accept if the chord's template is the best match and the cosine similarity is at or above 0.8 (tune with real recordings). Open strings with different tunings are a problem, so check tuning first (lesson 1 pass criterion).
7. Rhythm check (mic): detect onsets (spectral flux). Down versus up strokes cannot be distinguished reliably by audio, so score only onset times, not direction. A "hit" is an onset within +/-120 ms of the grid at 60 BPM early on, tightening to +/-80 ms by lesson 20. Pass = at least 75% of expected onsets hit and no more than 15% extra onsets. These numbers are design assumptions to calibrate, not research values.
8. Haptics: use the "N times" counts below as consecutive recognitions, not cumulative.

## 5. PROPOSED 30-LESSON SYLLABUS

**Conventions**
- Each lesson is about 12-18 min. Do one lesson per day; 5 days a week gives about 6 weeks, 30 days is the minimum.
- BPM refers to quarter-note click. "8ths" means two strums per click.
- "Pass" is the mic criterion that unlocks the next lesson. Chord pass: "chord recognised N of M attempts" where each attempt is a single clean strum after a 3-second hold of the shape, with a fresh placement each time (lift fingers between attempts).
- Review column: lessons that appear as 1-2 minute warm-ups later, on the schedule +1, +3, +7, +14 days from the first pass, using the lesson's content. Chord warm-ups from lessons that have their own R shown are interleaved (random order).
- Chord set is Em, Am, E, A, D, Dm, C, G (eight chords, the same count as JustinGuitar Grade 1). F and barre chords are intentionally outside this course.

Chord choices below are skeletons for the simple, folk versions. The existing arrangements in the app should be checked against these. The "target lesson" is when the chords of the song have been learned.

### Spaced-repetition schedule (summary)

- When a skill is passed for the first time, it enters a review queue at +1, +3, +7, +14 days. Each review is 1-2 minutes, with the chords interleaved in random order.
- If a review is failed (below 70%), the interval is reset to +1 day.
- Cumulative checkpoints: lessons 6, 14, 21, 30 retest everything learned.
- Adjustment: if a learner misses a day, do not fail the streak. Reduce today's new material and keep the review.

### Open items for the app developer

1. Calibrate recognition thresholds (cosine similarity, onset windows) using real recordings from different acoustic guitars and phone microphones. All numeric thresholds above are initial design assumptions.
2. Check the chords of each Hebrew song against the app's actual arrangement before locking the lesson order.
3. Decide whether to include F (easy version) in a second 30-lesson course.
4. A pilot with 3-5 real beginners is needed to validate the day-by-day pacing. No source supports a specific per-lesson pace.
